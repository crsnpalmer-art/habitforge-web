---Glycine doesn't get the attention of flashier compounds like NAD+ or NMN, but the research on this simple amino acid is quietly impressive. It's the smallest amino acid in the human body, found in high concentrations in collagen, and plays critical roles in sleep, inflammation, blood sugar regulation, and longevity. Despite being technically "non-essential" (the body can produce it), most people produce far less than they need — and the deficit shows.

## What Is Glycine?

Glycine is an amino acid that serves as both a building block for proteins and a neurotransmitter. It acts as an inhibitory neurotransmitter in the spinal cord and brainstem, helping calm neural activity — which is part of why it's so effective for sleep. It's also the primary amino acid in collagen (about 33% of collagen's structure), making it essential for joint, skin, and connective tissue health.

The problem: the body synthesizes roughly 3 grams of glycine per day, but researchers estimate optimal requirements closer to 10–15 grams daily. The gap is usually filled through dietary sources — primarily connective tissue, bone broth, and skin — which most people barely consume.

## Sleep Quality: The Core Benefit

The most well-studied benefit of glycine supplementation is sleep improvement. A landmark study published in *Sleep and Biological Rhythms* found that 3 grams of glycine taken before bed significantly improved subjective sleep quality, reduced daytime sleepiness, and improved cognitive performance the next morning — without changing sleep architecture in ways typical sleep aids do.

Mechanically, glycine lowers core body temperature, a key trigger for sleep onset. It does this by dilating blood vessels near the skin, accelerating heat dissipation. This mirrors what happens naturally in a healthy sleep cycle — and glycine essentially amplifies the effect.

Unlike melatonin or sedatives, glycine doesn't blunt REM sleep or leave users groggy. It works *with* the body's natural rhythm rather than overriding it.

## Longevity and Metabolic Health

Glycine is increasingly recognized as a longevity-relevant compound. Several mechanisms explain why:

**Methylation balance.** Glycine donates methyl groups in one-carbon metabolism, helping regulate DNA methylation — a process central to epigenetic aging. Inadequate glycine can accelerate biological aging at the cellular level.

**Glutathione synthesis.** Glycine is a precursor to glutathione, the body's master antioxidant. Older adults tend to have depleted glutathione levels, and glycine supplementation has been shown to restore them — with downstream benefits for oxidative stress and mitochondrial function.

**Insulin sensitivity.** Research has found glycine improves glucose disposal and insulin sensitivity, making it relevant for anyone managing blood sugar or metabolic syndrome. Glycine levels are inversely associated with type 2 diabetes risk in epidemiological studies.

**Inflammation.** Glycine inhibits activation of macrophages and reduces inflammatory cytokine production. Chronic low-grade inflammation is one of the primary drivers of aging diseases — glycine quietly dials it down.

## Collagen and Connective Tissue

Given that glycine makes up a third of collagen, supplementing it directly supports collagen synthesis — more efficiently than collagen peptides alone, which still need to be broken down and reassembled. For joint health, tendon recovery, skin elasticity, and gut lining integrity, ensuring adequate glycine intake is foundational.

## Dosage and How to Take It

- **For sleep:** 3 grams, 30–60 minutes before bed
- **For longevity/metabolic support:** 5–10 grams daily, split across meals
- **Form:** Powder is most economical; glycine has a mildly sweet taste and dissolves easily in water

Glycine is exceptionally safe — there's no established upper limit for dietary intake, and studies have used doses up to 60 grams daily without adverse effects.

## Who Should Consider Glycine

Glycine is a strong fit for anyone who:
- Struggles with sleep onset or sleep quality
- Eats a low-collagen diet (no bone broth, organ meat, or skin)
- Is focused on longevity protocols
- Has metabolic concerns or elevated blood sugar
- Experiences joint pain or slow recovery from training

It's one of the most cost-effective supplements on the market — a kilogram of bulk glycine powder costs less than most single-ingredient capsule supplements, and the evidence base rivals much more expensive longevity compounds.

## The Takeaway

Glycine is a quiet workhorse. It doesn't have a dramatic mechanism or a viral marketing story. What it has is consistent, replicated evidence across sleep, longevity, metabolism, and inflammation — at a dose and price point accessible to everyone. If you're already taking collagen but haven't looked at standalone glycine, it's worth adding to the stack.
